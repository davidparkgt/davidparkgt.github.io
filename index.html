About Me

I'm David Park, a Korean American engineering student who grew up speaking both Korean and English, and I carry that bilingual, bicultural background into the way I work with people. I began my engineering degree at the University of Georgia in 2024, where I earned a 3.8 GPA, and in August 2025 I transferred to Georgia Tech to pursue a Bachelor of Science in Electrical Engineering. I wanted to be in a place where the work is hard, the people are driven, and the focus on hardware is serious.

What interests me most is how a design on paper becomes something that actually runs. In Digital System Design I learned how combinational and sequential logic, finite state machines, and hardware description languages turn a set of rules into a working circuit. In Intro to Signal Processing I learned how sampling, filtering, and Fourier analysis let us pull meaning out of noisy signals. Both courses reward the same habit: break a messy problem into clean pieces, then check that the pieces fit back together.

I built that habit in places outside the classroom too. As a member of the Georgia Tech Esports varsity League of Legends team, I was the top-ranked candidate in the 2025 tryouts, and I help design team compositions, study patch changes, and review gameplay so we can adapt in real time. Before college, I ran the front of house at Danmooji Restaurant, a busy kitchen and dining room where I served 30+ guests at once and learned to stay calm and communicate clearly when everything happens at the same moment.

Put together, my story is about pressure, systems, and people. I like environments where decisions are fast, data matters, and a team has to trust each other. I am looking for an internship where I can apply my digital design and programming skills, learn from experienced engineers, and contribute to something real.

Career Goals

My long-term goal is to become an engineer who designs digital systems and connects the hardware to the software that controls it. I am interested in roles such as digital design, embedded systems, and hardware-software integration, where I can work on products that people depend on. Over time I also want to grow into a technical leader who can guide a team through fast decisions, which is a skill I already practice in competitive esports.

Here is my plan for getting from where I am now to where I want to be.

  1. Now – Spring 2027: Build the foundation

    Finish my core ECE courses with strong grades, deepen my Python, MATLAB, and Java skills, and keep my GPA competitive. Attend the ECE Career Fair with a sharp resume and a clear elevator pitch.

  2. May 2027: First internship

    Land an internship in digital design, embedded systems, or a related area. My goal is to test my classroom knowledge on real projects and learn how engineering teams work day to day.

  3. Junior and senior years: Specialize

    Take advanced courses in digital design, signal processing, and embedded systems, and add a project or research experience that shows I can build something end to end.

  4. After graduation: Launch

    Begin a full-time role as a hardware or hardware-software engineer, and consider a graduate degree if it helps me reach more specialized work.

Gaps I'm working on

I have strong coursework and teamwork experience, but I need more hands-on engineering projects and time in industry. To close that gap I plan to start a hardware or programming project each semester, share it on this site, and use GT KSEA's industry seminars and mentors to learn what employers look for.
