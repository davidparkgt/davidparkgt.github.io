01 · Introduction

About Me

My name is David Park, and I am pursuing a Bachelor of Science in Electrical Engineering at the Georgia Institute of Technology. I started college at the University of Georgia before transferring to Georgia Tech in 2025. Choosing to transfer was an important decision for me. I wanted to challenge myself in a stronger engineering environment, learn from classmates who take their work seriously, and be part of a school where many of my friends were also studying.

My interest in electrical engineering grew out of my longtime interest in computers. I have always found it interesting that so many different parts need to work together for a computer or device to do something that feels simple to the person using it. Electrical engineering gives me a way to understand some of those parts more deeply. I also see it as a field with a wide range of future opportunities, which matters to me as I think about building a stable and rewarding career.

I am still figuring out exactly where I want to specialize, and I think that is an important part of being a student. My academic threads are Robotics and Autonomous Systems and Electrical Systems, both of which give me ways to explore how electronics, control, programming, and physical devices connect. Courses in digital system design and signal processing have introduced me to ideas I want to understand better, and programming assignments have helped me practice turning instructions into something that works.

Outside of classes, I enjoy video games, cooking, traveling, and spending time outdoors. Competitive gaming has also become a major part of my college experience through Georgia Tech Esports. Playing on a team has taught me the importance of communication, preparation, and adjusting when a plan does not work. I also like getting outside and traveling when I can, because exploring new places helps me reset and gives me a different perspective. Cooking is another interest of mine, and I like the mix of practice, experimentation, and the satisfaction of getting a result I can share with other people.

I would describe myself as someone who is still learning what I am best at, but who is willing to take on new challenges. Through my courses, extracurricular activities, and future internships, I want to become more confident in both my technical abilities and my ability to contribute to a professional team.

02 · What I've worked on

Projects & Learning

I'm early in building my technical portfolio. Rather than listing projects I haven't completed, this section focuses on a robotics assignment I worked on and what it helped me learn.

CS 1301 Robotics Project

Fall 2025 · Python

Course project · Team programmer

For a CS 1301 project, I worked with a student team on programming a robot to move through its environment. My main responsibility was writing Python code for movement and obstacle avoidance. The goal was fairly straightforward to explain but required some care to implement: the robot needed to move, recognize when something was in its way, and avoid driving into walls or other obstacles.

The project gave me experience connecting programming to a physical result. Unlike a simple console program, the code had to make sense in the context of a moving device. I worked on movement routines and sensor-related decision logic that allowed the robot to respond to obstacles and adjust its path. Testing was important because even code that seemed reasonable needed to be checked against how the robot actually behaved.

I'm not presenting this as a large independent engineering build. It was a class team project, and my contribution was the programming side. Still, it was a useful introduction to the kind of work that interests me: using code to control hardware. It also gave me a reason to think more seriously about robotics as an area to explore through my ECE courses.

Digital System Design
